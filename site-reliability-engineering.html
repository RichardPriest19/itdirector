Site Reliability Engineering (SRE)

Applying software engineering discipline to operations, reliability, and incident management

Overview

Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) applies software engineering practices to operations problems, using service level objectives (SLOs), error budgets, and blameless postmortems to balance reliability against feature velocity. In 2026, SRE practice increasingly incorporates AI-assisted incident response, automated remediation (self-healing systems), and error-budget-driven release gating as standard parts of the discipline.

Key Value: SRE gives organizations a quantitative, shared framework — SLOs and error budgets — for deciding when to prioritize reliability work over new features, replacing subjective debates with data-driven trade-off decisions both engineering and business stakeholders can agree on.

Core Components

Core Practices

  • Service Level Objectives (SLOs) & error budgets
  • Blameless postmortems & incident review
  • Toil reduction through automation

Incident Management

  • On-call rotation design & escalation policies
  • Incident command structure for major outages
  • AI-assisted anomaly detection & triage

Reliability Engineering

  • Chaos engineering & failure injection testing
  • Capacity planning & load testing
  • Self-healing automation for common failure modes

Best Practices

  • Define SLOs collaboratively with product & business stakeholders
  • Use error budgets to gate release velocity, not just track uptime
  • Run blameless postmortems focused on systemic fixes, not blame
  • Automate toil systematically rather than accepting it as permanent
  • Practice failure scenarios proactively through chaos engineering

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Sponsor SRE practice adoption & SLO/error-budget governance
  • Ensure incident response & on-call structures are well-resourced
  • Track toil reduction & automation investment over time
  • Balance reliability investment against feature delivery priorities
