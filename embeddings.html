Embeddings

Turning text and other data into numerical vectors that capture meaning

Overview

Embeddings are numerical representations of data — most often text, but also images, audio or other content — as lists of numbers called vectors. The key idea is that these vectors capture meaning: items with similar meaning end up close together in the vector space, while unrelated items sit far apart. This lets computers compare concepts by similarity rather than by exact words, which is the foundation of semantic search, recommendation systems and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Embeddings are a quiet but essential building block of modern AI applications.

How They Work

An embedding model converts a piece of content into a vector of numbers — often hundreds or thousands of dimensions long. Because meaning is encoded as position in this space, you can measure how related two items are by measuring the distance between their vectors, using metrics such as cosine similarity. A search for "car" can then return results about "automobile" or "vehicle" even though the exact word never appears — because their embeddings are close. This is the difference between semantic search (by meaning) and traditional keyword search (by exact match).

What They Enable

  • Semantic search — find content by meaning rather than keywords.
  • RAG — retrieve the most relevant documents to ground an LLM's answers.
  • Recommendation — surface similar products, articles or media.
  • Clustering & classification — group or categorise large volumes of unstructured data.
  • Deduplication — detect near-duplicate or related content.

IT Director Considerations

  • Embeddings are the bridge between messy unstructured data and AI applications
  • Choice of embedding model affects quality, cost and language coverage
  • Cost and process of embedding large existing document estates
  • Re-embedding when you change models, since vectors are not comparable across different models
  • Where the embeddings are stored and searched — typically a vector database
  • Data sensitivity, since embedding involves sending content to a model

Embeddings, Vector Databases & RAG

These three concepts form a chain. Embeddings turn your content into meaning-bearing vectors. A vector database stores those vectors and searches them quickly by similarity. RAG uses that search to pull the most relevant content into an LLM's context so it can answer from your data. Embeddings are the first link: without them, there is nothing for the vector database to store or for RAG to retrieve.
