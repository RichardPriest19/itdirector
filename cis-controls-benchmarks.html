CIS Controls & Benchmarks

Prioritised security best practices and hardening baselines from the Center for Internet Security

Overview

The Center for Internet Security (CIS) is a non-profit that publishes two of the most practical, widely used resources in cybersecurity: the CIS Controls and the CIS Benchmarks. Where many frameworks describe what good security looks like in the abstract, CIS is prized for being concrete and actionable — a prioritised to-do list and detailed configuration guides that teams can apply directly. Both are free, consensus-developed by a global community of practitioners, and map cleanly to broader frameworks like NIST and ISO 27001.

CIS Controls

The CIS Controls are a prioritised set of safeguards to defend against the most common attacks. The current generation (version 8) consolidates them into 18 controls — covering essentials such as inventory of assets and software, data protection, secure configuration, access control, vulnerability management, logging and incident response. Their defining feature is prioritisation through Implementation Groups:

Group For
IG1 Essential cyber hygiene — the baseline every organisation should meet
IG2 Organisations managing more sensitive data and complexity
IG3 Mature organisations facing sophisticated, targeted threats

CIS Benchmarks

The CIS Benchmarks are detailed, consensus-based secure configuration guides for specific technologies — operating systems, cloud platforms, browsers, databases, network devices and more. There are hundreds of them, each prescribing how to harden a particular product against attack. CIS also publishes pre-hardened virtual machine images (CIS Hardened Images) for major cloud marketplaces, so teams can start from a secure baseline rather than hardening from scratch.

IT Director Considerations

  • A practical, prioritised roadmap — IG1 is an excellent starting point for "are we doing the basics?"
  • Benchmarks as ready-made hardening standards for your platforms
  • Mapping to NIST CSF, ISO 27001 and others, so CIS work supports formal compliance
  • Free to use, lowering the barrier compared with some commercial frameworks
  • Measurable: the Controls lend themselves to tracking and demonstrating progress
  • Tooling that can assess systems automatically against the benchmarks

CIS vs NIST & ISO

These are complementary, not competing. CIS is prescriptive and tactical — it tells you specifically what to do and how to configure systems. NIST frameworks (such as the Cybersecurity Framework and SP 800-53) and ISO 27001 are broader management frameworks describing how to structure and govern a security programme. A common pattern is to use NIST or ISO for the overall framework and certification, and CIS Controls and Benchmarks as the practical means of actually implementing and hardening.
