FISMA

US federal law requiring agencies and their contractors to secure government information systems

Overview

FISMA — the Federal Information Security Modernization Act (originally the Federal Information Security Management Act of 2002, updated in 2014) — is the US law that requires federal agencies to develop, document and operate a programme to secure their information and information systems. Crucially for the private sector, it also reaches contractors and service providers that handle federal data, so any organisation selling IT services to the US government is likely to encounter FISMA requirements. It is built directly on standards from NIST.

The NIST Foundation

FISMA compliance is achieved by following a set of NIST publications that together form the Risk Management Framework (RMF):

  • FIPS 199 & SP 800-60 — categorise each system's impact level as low, moderate or high.
  • FIPS 200 — set minimum security requirements.
  • SP 800-53 — the detailed catalogue of security and privacy controls to apply.
  • SP 800-37 — the Risk Management Framework process itself.

How Compliance Works

The RMF runs as a lifecycle: categorise the system by impact, select and implement the appropriate SP 800-53 controls, assess whether they are working, and then authorise the system for use. That authorisation — the Authority to Operate (ATO) — is a formal sign-off by a senior official accepting the residual risk. It is not a one-off: FISMA requires continuous monitoring and regular reporting to keep the authorisation valid. Oversight sits with the Office of Management and Budget and CISA.

IT Director Considerations

  • Directly relevant to federal agencies and to any vendor handling US government data
  • The ATO process can be lengthy and is a prerequisite to operating a system
  • Because it is built on SP 800-53, FISMA work overlaps heavily with other NIST-based programmes
  • Continuous monitoring is an ongoing operational obligation, not a one-time project
  • For cloud services, FedRAMP applies the same principles in a standardised, reusable way
  • Documentation and evidence are central — much of the effort is demonstrable assurance

FISMA & FedRAMP

FISMA and FedRAMP are closely linked. FISMA governs federal information systems in general; FedRAMP applies the same NIST-based approach specifically to cloud services used by the government, providing a standardised authorisation that can be reused across agencies. In short, FedRAMP is essentially FISMA for the cloud. Both ultimately rest on the NIST Risk Management Framework and SP 800-53 controls, so an organisation strong in one is well positioned for the other.
