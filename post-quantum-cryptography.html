Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)

Cryptographic algorithms designed to withstand attacks from cryptographically relevant quantum computers

Overview

Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) refers to cryptographic algorithms — mainly public-key schemes for key exchange and digital signatures — believed to be secure against attacks by both classical and quantum computers. In 2026, PQC has moved from standardization into active enterprise rollout, driven by NIST's finalized ML-KEM (Kyber), ML-DSA (Dilithium), and SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+) standards, hybrid classical/PQC deployments in browsers and TLS stacks, "harvest now, decrypt later" threat modeling, crypto-agility initiatives, and regulatory pressure (NSA CNSA 2.0, EU and UK government migration mandates) pushing organizations toward multi-year cryptographic transition roadmaps.

Key Value: PQC protects long-lived sensitive data and critical infrastructure against future quantum decryption threats, ensuring confidentiality and integrity of communications, signatures, and stored data well beyond the arrival of practical quantum computing.

Core Components (2026)

Standardized Algorithms

  • ML-KEM (Kyber) – key encapsulation for key exchange
  • ML-DSA (Dilithium) – primary digital signature scheme
  • SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+) – hash-based signature backup scheme

Transition Strategies

  • Hybrid classical + PQC key exchange (TLS 1.3 hybrid modes)
  • Crypto-agility – abstracted, swappable cryptographic libraries
  • Certificate & PKI migration planning

Risk Management

  • "Harvest now, decrypt later" data exposure assessment
  • Cryptographic inventory & bill of materials (CBOM)
  • Long-lived data prioritization for early migration

Key Trends & Best Practices in 2026

Enterprise Migration

  • Phased rollout starting with high-value, long-lived data
  • Hybrid PQC/classical deployment to de-risk transition
  • Vendor & cloud provider PQC readiness assessments

Governance & Compliance

  • Alignment with NIST, NSA CNSA 2.0, and regional mandates
  • Cryptographic asset inventories & lifecycle tracking
  • Third-party/supply-chain PQC compliance requirements

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Define enterprise PQC migration strategy & timeline
  • Build a cryptographic inventory across systems & vendors
  • Prioritize migration of long-lived, sensitive data stores
  • Ensure crypto-agility in new architecture decisions
  • Track vendor and cloud provider PQC roadmaps
  • Align with regulatory & industry compliance deadlines
