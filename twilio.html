Twilio

A cloud communications platform (CPaaS) for embedding messaging, voice and email into applications via APIs

Overview

Twilio is a cloud communications platform — a CPaaS (Communications Platform as a Service) — that lets organisations build messaging, voice, video and email directly into their own applications and workflows through programmable APIs. Founded in 2008, its model is "communications as building blocks": rather than buying a finished phone system, developers write code to send a text, place a call, verify a login or trigger a notification. This flexibility has made Twilio the backbone of communications for a vast range of apps, from appointment reminders to two-factor authentication codes.

How It Works

Twilio exposes its capabilities as web APIs and SDKs. An application makes a request — "send this SMS", "call this number" — and Twilio handles the underlying connectivity to carriers and networks worldwide. Incoming events (a reply, an inbound call) are delivered back to the application via webhooks, and call or message flows can be scripted. Pricing is usage-based: you pay per message, per minute, per phone number and so on, with no traditional fixed-line contracts. There is nothing to host — it is consumed entirely as a service.

Common Use Cases

  • Verification & 2FA — sending one-time login or transaction codes.
  • Notifications & alerts — order updates, appointment reminders, delivery tracking.
  • Customer engagement — marketing and conversational messaging across channels.
  • Contact centre — building or extending support operations with Flex.
  • Click-to-call & masked numbers — connecting users without exposing real numbers.

IT Director Considerations

  • Usage-based cost can scale quickly and unpredictably — model and monitor spend closely
  • Fraud risk such as SMS "pumping" and toll fraud, which can drive sudden costs
  • Messaging compliance — sender registration (e.g. A2P 10DLC in the US), consent/opt-in and anti-spam rules
  • Data privacy obligations (GDPR and, for some products, HIPAA eligibility)
  • It is a developer platform — building on it requires engineering resource
  • Protecting API credentials, and designing for reliability and deliverability

Twilio (CPaaS) vs UCaaS

It is easy to confuse Twilio with a phone or collaboration system, but they serve different needs. CPaaS like Twilio provides programmable building blocks to embed communications into your own applications and customer journeys — and requires development to use. UCaaS (unified communications as a service) provides ready-made calling, meetings and messaging for employees, out of the box. In short: use UCaaS to equip your staff to communicate, and CPaaS like Twilio to build communications into the products and processes your customers interact with. Many organisations use both.
