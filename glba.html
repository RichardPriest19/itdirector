GLBA

US law requiring financial institutions to protect and respect the privacy of customer information

Overview

The Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), passed in 1999, requires US financial institutions to protect the security and confidentiality of their customers' personal financial information and to be transparent about how it is shared. Its definition of "financial institution" is broad — covering not just banks and insurers but also entities such as mortgage brokers, tax preparers, debt collectors and others significantly engaged in financial activities. For IT leaders in any such organisation, GLBA is a primary driver of data-protection obligations.

The Key Rules

Rule Requirement
Financial Privacy Rule Give privacy notices and let customers opt out of certain data sharing
Safeguards Rule Maintain a documented information security programme
Pretexting provisions Guard against social-engineering attempts to obtain customer information

The Safeguards Rule in Focus

The Safeguards Rule is the part most relevant to IT. It requires a written information security programme with administrative, technical and physical controls. The Federal Trade Commission strengthened it significantly in recent years, adding far more specific expectations — including designating a qualified individual to run the programme, conducting risk assessments, and implementing concrete measures such as encryption of customer data, multi-factor authentication, access controls, logging and monitoring, and an incident response plan. This moved GLBA from broad principles toward prescriptive security requirements.

IT Director Considerations

  • Confirm whether your organisation meets the broad definition of a financial institution
  • The Safeguards Rule effectively mandates a formal, documented security programme
  • Specific technical controls now expected: encryption, MFA, access control and monitoring
  • A named, accountable individual responsible for the security programme
  • Vendor and third-party oversight, since service providers handle covered data
  • Incident response readiness as a regulatory expectation, not just good practice

GLBA in Context

GLBA is the financial sector's counterpart to other sector-specific US regimes — much as HIPAA governs health information and FERPA governs education records. It pairs a privacy dimension (notices and sharing choices) with a security dimension (the Safeguards Rule). For organisations also subject to broader privacy laws or security frameworks, a single well-run information security programme — aligned to something like the CIS Controls or NIST — can satisfy much of GLBA alongside those other obligations.
