FERPA

US law protecting the privacy of student education records

Overview

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), enacted in 1974, is the US federal law that protects the privacy of students' education records. It applies to schools, colleges and universities that receive funding from the US Department of Education — which is to say, the great majority of them. FERPA gives students and parents rights over those records and restricts how institutions may disclose them. For IT leaders in the education sector, it shapes how systems holding student data — from student information systems to learning platforms — must be designed and governed.

The Rights It Grants

FERPA gives rights to parents, which transfer to the student once they turn 18 or enter higher education (becoming an "eligible student"):

  • Inspect and review the student's education records held by the institution.
  • Request correction of records believed to be inaccurate or misleading.
  • Control disclosure of personally identifiable information from the records, with limited exceptions.

Disclosure & Consent

As a rule, an institution must have written consent before disclosing personally identifiable information from a student's education records. FERPA allows specific exceptions — for example, disclosure to school officials with a legitimate educational interest, to another school to which a student is transferring, or for health and safety emergencies. So-called directory information (such as name or enrolment status) may be disclosed without consent, but only if the institution has given notice and an opportunity to opt out. Enforcement rests with the US Department of Education, and the ultimate sanction is loss of federal funding.

IT Director Considerations

  • Applies across the systems holding student data — SIS, LMS, email, assessment and more
  • Access controls that enforce "legitimate educational interest" — staff see only what they need
  • Third-party edtech vendors: contracts and data handling must keep the institution compliant
  • Configuration of directory-information settings and opt-out handling
  • Audit trails of who accessed or disclosed records
  • Where students are minors, the interaction with other child-privacy rules

FERPA in Context

FERPA is the education sector's equivalent of other sector-specific US privacy regimes — HIPAA for health information, GLBA for financial data. Its focus is privacy and disclosure of records rather than prescriptive cybersecurity, so institutions typically meet it by combining FERPA's disclosure rules with a general security framework (such as the CIS Controls or NIST) to protect the underlying systems. For education organisations, FERPA is usually the starting point around which wider data-governance and security practices are built.
