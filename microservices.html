Microservices

Building applications as independently deployable services organized around business capability

Overview

Microservices architecture decomposes applications into small, independently deployable services, each owning a specific business capability and communicating over well-defined APIs. In 2026, mature microservices practice emphasizes service mesh for cross-cutting concerns, contract-based API testing, and domain-driven design boundaries — with growing recognition that microservices trade development-time simplicity for operational complexity, making the decision workload-dependent rather than default.

Key Value: Microservices let teams deploy, scale, and evolve services independently, enabling faster iteration and better fault isolation — but only when organizational structure, tooling maturity, and workload characteristics justify the added operational complexity.

Core Components

Design Principles

  • Domain-driven design & bounded contexts
  • API-first contract design between services
  • Database-per-service data ownership

Operational Foundations

  • Service mesh for traffic management & observability
  • Distributed tracing across service boundaries
  • Independent CI/CD pipelines per service

Resilience Patterns

  • Circuit breakers & graceful degradation
  • Contract testing to prevent breaking changes
  • Centralized API gateway for external access

Best Practices

  • Only decompose into microservices where team structure & scale justify it
  • Establish clear service ownership & on-call accountability
  • Invest in distributed tracing before scaling service count
  • Use contract testing to catch breaking changes between services
  • Avoid distributed monoliths — ensure services are truly independently deployable

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Assess whether microservices architecture fits organizational maturity
  • Ensure observability & tracing scale with service count
  • Define service ownership & governance standards
  • Monitor for accidental complexity (distributed monolith anti-patterns)
