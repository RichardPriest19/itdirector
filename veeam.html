Veeam

Software-defined backup and data protection across virtual, physical, cloud and SaaS workloads

Overview

Veeam is one of the most widely used backup and data-protection vendors, consistently ranked among the market leaders. It made its name protecting virtual machines — particularly VMware — with a product known for being reliable and genuinely easy to use, and has since broadened to cover physical servers, cloud workloads (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), SaaS applications such as Microsoft 365, and Kubernetes. A defining trait is that Veeam is software-only and hardware-agnostic: you run it on the infrastructure and storage of your choice, rather than buying a Veeam appliance.

Key Capabilities

  • Backup & replication — image-based backup with fast, granular recovery.
  • Instant recovery — run a workload directly from a backup to minimise downtime.
  • Immutability — hardened repositories and object-lock so backups cannot be altered or deleted by ransomware.
  • Microsoft 365 backup — protect Exchange, SharePoint, OneDrive and Teams data.
  • Cloud & SaaS coverage — native protection for major cloud and SaaS platforms.
  • Monitoring — visibility and reporting across the data-protection estate.

Why It's Popular

Veeam's appeal rests on flexibility and simplicity. Being software-only avoids lock-in to a particular storage vendor and lets organisations reuse existing hardware. Its strong virtualisation heritage, broad workload support and reputation for straightforward recovery make it a common default, particularly in mid-market and enterprise environments built on VMware and Microsoft.

IT Director Considerations

  • The value of hardware-agnostic software for avoiding storage lock-in
  • Immutability and air-gapped copies as core ransomware defences
  • SaaS backup — Microsoft does not fully back up your Microsoft 365 data for you
  • Licensing model (per-workload / instance, subscription-based) and how it scales
  • Regular, tested recovery — backups are only as good as your ability to restore
  • How it fits the wider disaster-recovery and business-continuity strategy

Veeam vs Commvault, Rubrik & Cohesity

Veeam is flexible, software-only and rooted in virtualisation, prized for ease of use and broad coverage. Commvault is a long-established, comprehensive enterprise platform with deep data-management capabilities. Rubrik is a newer, security-led entrant focused on cyber resilience and fast ransomware recovery. Cohesity emphasises consolidating secondary data at web scale and, following its combination with Veritas' enterprise data protection business, is now one of the largest players. All four increasingly converge on the same priorities — immutability, anti-ransomware and rapid recovery — so the choice often comes down to existing estate, scale and whether you prefer software-only or an integrated platform.
