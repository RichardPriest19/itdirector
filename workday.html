Workday

Cloud-based enterprise platform for human capital management and financial management

Overview

Workday is a cloud-based (SaaS) enterprise application suite best known for human capital management (HCM) and financial management. Founded in 2005 by the former leadership of PeopleSoft, it was built cloud-native from the start, and is aimed primarily at medium-to-large and enterprise organisations — with particular strength in services-led sectors such as professional services, education, healthcare, technology and finance. Its core proposition is a single, unified system for managing an organisation's people and money, delivered as a continuously updated cloud service.

The Two Pillars

Workday HCM Workday Financial Management
Core HR and worker records General ledger and accounting
Payroll, benefits, time tracking Procurement and expenses
Recruiting, talent and learning Revenue and financial reporting
Workforce planning Planning & analytics (Adaptive Planning)

Around these sit additional capabilities such as spend management, analytics (Prism), employee engagement, and Workday Extend for building custom applications on the platform.

How It Works

Workday runs as a single, multi-tenant cloud service — there is no on-premises version. All customers run on one current version, and Workday delivers regular feature updates (typically a couple of major releases a year) rather than the disruptive upgrade cycles of traditional on-premises software. The platform favours configuration over customisation: organisations tailor behaviour through settings rather than altering code, which keeps them on the upgrade path. A mobile-first interface and built-in analytics are central to its design.

Strengths

  • Unified people and finance data — one system rather than separate HR and finance silos.
  • User experience — a modern, consumer-grade and mobile-friendly interface.
  • Continuous innovation — frequent updates with no major re-implementation.
  • Analytics and planning — reporting and FP&A built into the platform.
  • Configurability — adapt without custom code, easing maintenance and upgrades.

IT Director Considerations

  • SaaS model removes infrastructure to run, but increases reliance on the vendor
  • Implementation is a significant programme — usually partner-led, with real cost and change effort
  • Twice-yearly updates require a regression-testing routine, even though upgrades are smooth
  • Integration with the rest of the estate via Workday's APIs and integration tooling
  • Configuration governance to avoid sprawl and keep the system maintainable
  • Data security, privacy and residency for sensitive HR and financial data
  • Emerging AI features — promising, but worth evaluating on current, verified capability

Workday vs SAP, Oracle & Dynamics

Workday is cloud-native and unifies HCM and financials with a strong emphasis on user experience and services-led industries. SAP and Oracle offer broader, deeper ERP — including strong manufacturing and supply-chain capabilities — and compete in HCM via SAP SuccessFactors and Oracle Fusion HCM. Microsoft Dynamics 365 appeals where tight integration with the Microsoft ecosystem matters. The decision often turns on industry fit and what dominates the requirement: Workday tends to win where people and finance are the heart of the business and a clean cloud experience is a priority, while the broader ERP suites lead where complex operations and supply chains dominate.
