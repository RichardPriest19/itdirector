Scrum

Scrum framework – roles, ceremonies, artifacts & empirical process control

Overview

Scrum is a lightweight framework for managing iterative, incremental product development through defined roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team), time-boxed events (sprints, daily standups, reviews, retrospectives), and artifacts (product backlog, sprint backlog, increment). In 2026, Scrum remains the most widely used agile framework globally, though many teams run it in hybrid forms — Scrumban, SAFe-integrated Scrum — adapted to their specific delivery context rather than by-the-book implementation.

Key Value: Scrum's defined roles, ceremonies, and empirical inspect-and-adapt cycle give teams a structured, well-documented starting point for iterative delivery — its widespread adoption also means abundant coaching, tooling, and community support compared to bespoke process design.

Core Components

Roles

  • Product Owner — backlog ownership & prioritization
  • Scrum Master — process facilitation & impediment removal
  • Development Team — self-organizing delivery

Events

  • Sprint planning, daily standup, sprint review, retrospective
  • Time-boxed sprints for regular, predictable delivery cadence
  • Backlog refinement for ongoing prioritization

Artifacts

  • Product backlog — prioritized feature/requirement list
  • Sprint backlog — committed work for the current sprint
  • Increment — the potentially shippable output of each sprint

Best Practices

  • Keep sprint length consistent to build a predictable delivery rhythm
  • Protect the Scrum Master's role as a facilitator, not a project manager giving orders
  • Use retrospectives genuinely for process improvement, not just a formality
  • Adapt Scrum pragmatically (Scrumban, SAFe integration) rather than dogmatic by-the-book adherence
  • Keep the product backlog realistically prioritized and regularly refined

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Support Scrum framework adoption & continuous improvement across teams
  • Ensure Scrum Master & Product Owner roles are properly resourced & protected
  • Track sprint delivery predictability & retrospective action follow-through
  • Allow teams to adapt Scrum pragmatically to their actual delivery context
