Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA)

A structured process for identifying and mitigating privacy risks before deploying a new system

Overview

A Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) — also called a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) under GDPR — is a structured process for identifying, evaluating, and mitigating privacy risks before a new system, process, or data use begins, particularly when processing is likely to result in high risk to individuals. In 2026, PIA processes increasingly include specific assessment criteria for AI/ML systems, evaluating risks around automated decision-making, profiling, and training data provenance alongside traditional data processing concerns.

Key Value: A rigorous PIA process catches privacy risks — excessive data collection, inadequate safeguards, unclear legal basis — while a system is still on the drawing board, when fixing them costs a design change rather than a costly post-launch remediation or regulatory penalty.

Core Components

Assessment Process

  • Data flow mapping & processing purpose documentation
  • Necessity & proportionality assessment
  • Risk identification & mitigation planning

Trigger Criteria

  • Large-scale processing of sensitive personal data
  • Systematic monitoring or profiling of individuals
  • New technology deployment with unclear privacy implications

AI-Specific Considerations

  • Automated decision-making & profiling risk assessment
  • Training data provenance & consent basis review
  • Algorithmic bias & fairness risk evaluation

Best Practices

  • Trigger a PIA automatically for high-risk processing activities, not just on request
  • Complete PIAs before development begins, not as a launch-gate afterthought
  • Document the legal basis & necessity for each data processing purpose
  • Extend PIA criteria explicitly to cover AI/ML automated decision-making risks
  • Treat identified risks as requiring documented mitigation, not just acknowledgment

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Ensure PIA/DPIA processes are triggered consistently for qualifying projects
  • Integrate PIA completion into project approval gates
  • Extend PIA criteria to address AI-specific privacy risks
  • Coordinate PIA outcomes with legal & data protection officer review
