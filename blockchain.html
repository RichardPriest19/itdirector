Blockchain & Distributed Ledger Technology

Decentralized, tamper-evident ledgers for scenarios requiring shared trust across organizations

Overview

Blockchain and distributed ledger technology provide a decentralized, tamper-evident record shared across multiple parties without requiring a single trusted central authority. In 2026, enterprise blockchain use has narrowed from the broad experimentation of prior years to specific, well-justified use cases — supply chain provenance, multi-party settlement, and digital asset/identity credentials — where decentralized trust genuinely solves a problem a centralized database cannot.

Key Value: Blockchain's value is specific and narrow — it solves the problem of establishing trust and shared state among parties that don't fully trust each other or a common intermediary — making it a poor fit for the majority of enterprise data problems that a well-governed centralized database already handles more simply and cheaply.

Core Components

Use Case Fit

  • Multi-party supply chain provenance tracking
  • Cross-organization settlement & reconciliation
  • Verifiable digital credentials & identity

Technical Considerations

  • Public vs. permissioned/private ledger selection
  • Smart contract security & audit requirements
  • Interoperability between ledger platforms

Governance

  • Consortium governance for multi-party ledgers
  • Regulatory treatment of digital assets & tokens
  • Realistic ROI assessment against simpler alternatives

Best Practices

  • Validate that decentralized trust is genuinely required before choosing blockchain over a centralized database
  • Use permissioned ledgers for enterprise consortium use cases requiring accountability
  • Audit smart contracts rigorously — bugs there are often irreversible
  • Establish clear multi-party governance before launching a consortium ledger
  • Reassess existing blockchain pilots against whether they've delivered genuine value

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Evaluate blockchain use cases against simpler centralized alternatives first
  • Ensure smart contract security auditing before production deployment
  • Establish governance structure for any multi-party consortium ledger
  • Track realistic ROI rather than treating blockchain as inherently strategic
