Threat Intelligence

Collecting and analyzing information on threat actors, campaigns, and indicators to inform defense

Overview

Threat intelligence is the collection, analysis, and dissemination of information about threat actors, their tactics, and indicators of compromise (IOCs) to inform proactive and reactive security decisions. In 2026, enterprise threat intelligence programs integrate strategic (executive-level), operational (campaign-level), and tactical (IOC-level) intelligence feeds, increasingly using AI to correlate and prioritize the flood of raw indicator data into actionable findings.

Key Value: Well-integrated threat intelligence turns generic security alerts into context-rich decisions — knowing which threat actors target your industry, and what their known tactics look like, sharpens both detection rules and executive risk conversations.

Core Components

Intelligence Tiers

  • Strategic intelligence for executive & board risk decisions
  • Operational intelligence on campaigns & threat actor TTPs
  • Tactical intelligence — IOCs feeding detection rules

Sources & Sharing

  • Commercial & open-source threat intelligence feeds
  • Industry ISAC/ISAO information sharing communities
  • Internal telemetry correlated with external intelligence

Program Operations

  • Intelligence requirements definition tied to business risk
  • Analyst tooling for correlation & enrichment
  • Dissemination to SOC, IR & leadership stakeholders

Best Practices

  • Define intelligence requirements tied to your actual threat landscape, not generic feeds
  • Integrate threat intel directly into detection & SOC workflows
  • Participate in relevant industry ISAC/ISAO sharing communities
  • Separate strategic, operational & tactical intelligence for the right audiences
  • Regularly assess feed quality — volume of IOCs is not the same as value

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Sponsor threat intelligence program & platform investment
  • Ensure intelligence integrates with SOC & detection engineering
  • Support participation in industry information-sharing communities
  • Use strategic intelligence to inform board-level risk reporting
