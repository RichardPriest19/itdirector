Penetration Testing

Authorized, simulated attacks used to identify exploitable weaknesses before real attackers do

Overview

Penetration testing is authorized, simulated attack activity conducted by security professionals to identify exploitable vulnerabilities in systems, networks, and applications before malicious actors find them. In 2026, enterprise programs combine periodic comprehensive assessments with continuous automated testing, cloud-specific penetration testing methodologies, and increasingly AI-assisted attack surface discovery to keep pace with fast-changing environments.

Key Value: Regular, well-scoped penetration testing validates that security controls actually work under realistic attack conditions, converting theoretical risk assessments into concrete, prioritized findings that map directly to remediation work.

Core Components

Testing Types

  • Network & infrastructure penetration testing
  • Web & mobile application penetration testing
  • Cloud configuration & IAM penetration testing

Program Structure

  • Scoping & rules of engagement definition
  • Internal red team vs. third-party engagement decisions
  • Continuous/automated testing between full assessments

Remediation

  • Risk-based prioritization of findings
  • Retesting to confirm remediation effectiveness
  • Executive & board-level reporting of results

Best Practices

  • Scope penetration tests to reflect realistic attacker objectives, not just a checklist
  • Combine periodic deep assessments with continuous automated testing
  • Prioritize remediation by exploitability & business impact, not just severity score
  • Retest confirmed fixes before closing findings
  • Rotate testing vendors/teams periodically for fresh perspective

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Sponsor a recurring penetration testing program & budget
  • Ensure findings are prioritized, tracked, and remediated on schedule
  • Report significant findings & trends to executive leadership
  • Balance internal red team capability against third-party engagement needs

Resources

  • NIST SP 800-115 Technical Guide to Information Security Testing
  • PTES (Penetration Testing Execution Standard)
  • OWASP Testing Guide
  • CREST & OSCP penetration testing certification bodies
