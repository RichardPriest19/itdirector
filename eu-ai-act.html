EU AI Act Compliance

The European Union's risk-based regulatory framework governing the development and use of AI systems

Overview

The EU AI Act is the European Union's comprehensive, risk-based regulatory framework for artificial intelligence, classifying AI systems into unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal risk tiers with correspondingly graduated obligations. In 2026, phased obligations under the Act are actively taking effect, with prohibited-practice rules and general-purpose AI model obligations already binding, and high-risk system requirements phasing in on their own timeline — making active compliance tracking essential rather than optional for organizations deploying AI in or affecting the EU market.

Key Value: The EU AI Act's extraterritorial reach means it applies to many organizations outside the EU that deploy AI systems affecting EU residents, making proactive risk classification and documentation essential well before an organization's own market presence might suggest urgency.

Core Components

Risk Classification

  • Unacceptable risk — prohibited AI practices
  • High-risk systems — conformity assessment & documentation obligations
  • Limited & minimal risk — transparency obligations

Compliance Obligations

  • Technical documentation & risk management systems for high-risk AI
  • Transparency requirements for user-facing AI & generative systems
  • General-purpose AI model provider obligations

Governance & Enforcement

  • EU AI Office & national supervisory authority oversight
  • Phased implementation timeline by obligation category
  • Penalties scaled to global annual turnover for serious violations

Best Practices

  • Classify every AI system in use or development against the Act's risk tiers now, not at enforcement deadlines
  • Maintain technical documentation & risk management records for any high-risk system
  • Track the phased implementation timeline — obligations activate on different schedules by category
  • Assess extraterritorial applicability even for organizations without an EU legal entity
  • Coordinate compliance tracking with the broader AI governance & model governance programs

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Sponsor an EU AI Act compliance program & risk classification exercise
  • Ensure documentation obligations are met for any high-risk AI system in use
  • Track the phased obligation timeline & upcoming compliance deadlines
  • Coordinate with legal counsel on extraterritorial applicability & enforcement exposure
