TOGAF

The Open Group Architecture Framework – a method for developing and governing enterprise architecture

Overview

TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) is the most widely adopted enterprise architecture framework. Maintained by The Open Group, its defining contribution is a method — a structured, repeatable process for developing, governing and evolving an architecture, rather than just a set of diagrams. It gives architects across an organisation a common language and a consistent way of working, which is much of why it has become an industry standard and a popular professional certification.

The ADM

At the heart of TOGAF is the Architecture Development Method (ADM) — a cyclical process that takes architecture from initial vision through to implementation and ongoing change. Its phases run roughly as follows, with Requirements Management sitting at the centre throughout:

Phase Focus
Preliminary Set up the architecture capability and principles
A — Vision Define scope, stakeholders and the target vision
B, C, D Business, Information Systems and Technology architectures
E — Opportunities & Solutions Identify delivery options and group into projects
F — Migration Planning Sequence the work into a roadmap
G — Implementation Governance Oversee delivery against the architecture
H — Change Management Manage ongoing change and feed the next cycle

Other Key Components

  • Architecture Content Framework — defines the deliverables, artefacts and building blocks the ADM produces.
  • Enterprise Continuum — a way of classifying solutions from generic to organisation-specific.
  • Architecture Repository — where standards, reference models and assets are stored and reused.
  • Architecture Governance — the structures that keep delivery aligned to the architecture.

Certification

TOGAF certification is well established and widely sought for enterprise architect roles, typically structured as a Foundation level (knowledge of terminology and concepts) and a higher Certified level (applying the framework). For organisations, certification provides a shared baseline of understanding; for individuals, it is a recognised credential.

IT Director Considerations

  • A common method and vocabulary that aligns architects across the organisation
  • The risk of applying TOGAF too rigidly — it is explicitly meant to be tailored, not followed slavishly
  • "Just enough architecture": adapting the ADM so it guides rather than slows delivery
  • Certification as a way to build a consistent skills baseline in the team
  • How TOGAF coexists with agile delivery rather than gatekeeping it
  • Using TOGAF's process alongside a classification approach such as Zachman for the outputs

TOGAF vs Zachman

TOGAF answers how — it provides a step-by-step method (the ADM) for developing and governing architecture over time. Zachman answers what — it provides a taxonomy for classifying and organising architectural artefacts, but no process for producing them. They are complementary: TOGAF drives the work, Zachman can structure and check the completeness of the outputs. Both sit within the broader discipline of enterprise architecture.
