Kanban

A visual workflow management method limiting work in progress to improve flow

Overview

Kanban is a visual workflow management method that represents work as cards moving through columns representing process stages, with explicit work-in-progress (WIP) limits used to identify bottlenecks and improve flow. In 2026, Kanban remains widely used both as a standalone lean methodology and combined with Scrum (Scrumban) for teams managing a mix of planned sprint work and continuous, unscheduled inbound requests such as support tickets.

Key Value: Kanban's visual, WIP-limited approach surfaces bottlenecks immediately and suits teams handling continuous, unpredictable inbound work — such as support or operations teams — better than fixed-length sprint cycles designed for planned feature delivery.

Core Components

Core Practices

  • Visual board representing workflow stages
  • Explicit work-in-progress (WIP) limits per column
  • Pull-based work intake rather than push scheduling

Flow Metrics

  • Cycle time & lead time measurement
  • Cumulative flow diagrams for bottleneck identification
  • Throughput tracking over time

Team Application

  • Support & operations team ticket flow management
  • Scrumban for hybrid sprint/continuous-flow teams
  • Class-of-service policies for expedited work

Best Practices

  • Set WIP limits based on actual team capacity, not aspiration
  • Use cycle time and cumulative flow data to identify real bottlenecks
  • Apply class-of-service policies for genuinely urgent/expedited work
  • Review flow metrics regularly rather than only at retrospectives
  • Combine with Scrum (Scrumban) for teams with both planned & reactive work

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Introduce Kanban for teams managing continuous, unscheduled work
  • Ensure WIP limits are respected & enforced, not just displayed
  • Track flow metrics to identify systemic delivery bottlenecks
  • Support teams in choosing Kanban, Scrum, or a hybrid based on actual work patterns
