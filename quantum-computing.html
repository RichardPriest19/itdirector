Quantum Computing

An emerging computing paradigm using quantum mechanics to solve certain problems exponentially faster

Overview

Quantum computing uses quantum mechanical phenomena — superposition and entanglement — to perform certain classes of computation dramatically faster than classical computers, with promising applications in optimization, materials simulation, and cryptography. In 2026, quantum computing remains largely in the experimentation and early pilot phase for most enterprises, with cloud-based quantum computing access from major providers making exploration accessible without capital investment in specialized hardware.

Key Value: While practical, large-scale quantum advantage for most business problems remains years away, enterprises exploring quantum computing today build institutional expertise and identify use cases early — while quantum's more immediate and urgent implication, breaking current public-key cryptography, is already driving the Post-Quantum Cryptography migration underway across the industry.

Core Components

Access Models

  • Cloud-based quantum computing access (quantum-as-a-service)
  • Quantum simulators for algorithm development without hardware
  • Hybrid classical-quantum computing architectures

Current Applications

  • Optimization problems (logistics, portfolio, scheduling)
  • Materials science & drug discovery simulation
  • Cryptographic research & post-quantum algorithm validation

Enterprise Readiness

  • Talent & skills development for quantum algorithms
  • Use case identification & pilot project selection
  • Coordination with post-quantum cryptography migration planning

Best Practices

  • Treat current quantum computing investment as exploration & capability building, not production deployment
  • Prioritize the parallel post-quantum cryptography migration as the more urgent near-term action
  • Use cloud-based quantum access to experiment without hardware capital investment
  • Identify specific optimization or simulation use cases before committing significant resources
  • Build internal expertise gradually rather than over-investing ahead of practical need

IT Director Responsibilities

  • Monitor quantum computing maturity relevant to your industry's use cases
  • Prioritize post-quantum cryptography migration as the more immediate action
  • Sponsor limited exploratory pilots where a clear use case exists
  • Track talent & skills development needs for future quantum readiness
